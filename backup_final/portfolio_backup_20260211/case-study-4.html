04 EVENT DISCOVERY GAP ANALYSIS

WHAT ARE USERS SEARCHING FOR THAT WE DON'T OFFER?

This analysis uses text mining to identify high-volume search themes with insufficient event supply. By clustering 47,000 monthly search queries and mapping them to existing categories, we surface "white space" opportunities where user intent goes unmet—revealing what new event types to prioritize.

METHOD: TEXT MINING + DEMAND CLASSIFICATION

Why Text Mining (Not Simple Search Counts)?

Previous approach (inadequate): Count searches without matching events
Problem: Tells us that there's unmet demand but not what users actually want. Can't prioritize or design supply strategy.

Improved approach chosen:

  • Step 1: Cluster search queries using NLP (TF-IDF vectorization + K-means clustering)
  • Step 2: Label clusters by dominant themes (e.g., "wellness + music", "silent disco", "rooftop venue")
  • Step 3: Map clusters to existing event categories
  • Step 4: Identify "orphan clusters" — searches with no matching supply
  • Step 5: Size opportunity by: search volume × estimated conversion rate × average ticket price

Why This Method Is Better

  • Reveals user intent: Not just "1,200 searches unmet" but "1,200 searches specifically for rooftop venues with electronic music"
  • Enables prioritization: Can rank gaps by volume × feasibility × conversion potential
  • Surfaces latent needs: Discovers categories users want that don't currently exist (e.g., "wellness + music" hybrid events)
  • Actionable outputs: Tells promoters what to create, not just "create more events"

Technical Implementation

Data source: 47,000 search queries from past 3 months (deduplicated by user)

Text preprocessing:

  • Lowercased, removed stop words ("the", "a", "in")
  • Stemmed words to root forms ("dancing" → "dance")
  • Filtered out location-only queries ("London events" too generic)

Clustering approach:

  • TF-IDF vectorization (captures word importance, not just frequency)
  • K-means with k=25 clusters (elbow method suggested 20-30 optimal)
  • Manual review of cluster examples to assign meaningful labels

Limitation: Clustering Requires Judgment

K-means doesn't automatically label clusters—requires analyst interpretation. For example, cluster containing "yoga music", "meditation DJ", "sound healing" was labeled "Wellness + Music" based on dominant themes. Different analysts might label slightly differently, introducing subjectivity.

Mitigation: Validated labels with 3 team members independently reviewing samples. 85% agreement rate confirms reasonable consistency.

KEY INSIGHTS

Finding 1: "Wellness + Music" — High-Volume Zero-Supply Gap

Search volume: 892 monthly queries
Current supply: 3 events (gap ratio 297:1)

User intent (from query analysis):

  • Yoga sessions with live ambient/electronic music
  • Meditation experiences with DJ/producer performances
  • Sound healing workshops in club/venue settings
  • "Sober raves" (wellness-oriented dance events, no alcohol)

Demographic signal (from user profiles who searched):

  • 73% female, age 25-40
  • Higher income bracket (£45K+ median vs £32K platform average)
  • Also search for: boutique fitness, organic food, mindfulness content
  • Historical ticket spend: £40-60 range for experiential events

Opportunity sizing:
892 searches/month × 15% conversion (conservative) × £45 avg ticket = £6,021 monthly revenue = £72K annual
