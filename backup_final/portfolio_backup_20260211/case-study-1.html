01 GEOGRAPHIC DEMAND HEATMAP ANALYSIS

WHERE IS USER DEMAND OUTPACING EVENT SUPPLY?

This analysis maps user search behavior against event listings across UK cities to identify underserved markets. By calculating demand-to-supply ratios, we reveal where geographical expansion would capture the highest latent demand with the lowest competitive intensity.

METHOD: DEMAND/SUPPLY RATIO ANALYSIS

Why This Method?

Chosen because: Ratio-based approach normalizes across cities of different sizes, revealing relative underservice rather than absolute volume gaps. A city with 100 searches and 5 events (20:1 ratio) is more underserved than a city with 500 searches and 50 events (10:1), even though the latter has higher absolute search volume.

Alternative considered: Absolute search volume ranking
Rejected because: Would bias recommendations toward large cities (London, Manchester), missing high-growth opportunities in mid-sized markets where we could establish early-mover advantage.

Limitation: Assumes search intent converts at similar rates across geographies. This may not hold if Birmingham users search differently (more browsing, less buying) than London users. Conversion rate analysis would validate this assumption but requires transaction data not yet available.

KEY INSIGHTS

  • Birmingham shows 22.1× demand/supply ratio – 17% above platform average (18.9×), indicating systematic undersupply that has persisted for 6+ months across multiple genres
  • Pattern is consistent, not noise: Gap has remained stable through seasonal variations, suggesting structural undersupply rather than temporary demand spike
  • London Techno presents different opportunity: High absolute demand (2,340 monthly searches) but only 20 events listed. Need for 117 additional events to reach platform average ratio – but higher competitive intensity
  • Manchester and Edinburgh show similar patterns – both 19-20× ratios, suggesting coordinated regional strategy could work

Critical Caveat: Correlation ≠ Causation

High search-to-event ratios correlate with undersupply, but we cannot conclude this proves latent demand without testing. Alternative explanations include:

  • Low event quality: Users search repeatedly because existing events don't appeal (supply exists but isn't desirable)
  • Price sensitivity: Users browse but don't convert at prevailing prices (demand exists but not at current price points)
  • Genre mismatch: Searches may be for event types not in our catalog (e.g., "classical concerts" when we only list electronic music)

Next step before scaling: Run targeted experiments in Birmingham to validate that supply additions actually convert searchers into buyers.

STRATEGIC RECOMMENDATIONS

Immediate Priority: Birmingham Pilot (Test, Don't Bet)

  • Phase 1 (8 weeks): Partner with 3-5 Birmingham promoters for 20 events
    • Track: Search-to-ticket conversion rate, repeat booking rate, customer acquisition cost
    • Success criteria: >12% conversion (vs 8% platform avg), <£15 CAC,>25% repeat rate
    • Investment: ~£8K (partnership fees + localized marketing)
  • If successful: Scale to 100+ annual Birmingham events (£127K revenue opportunity)
  • If unsuccessful: Investigate search intent mismatch before further investment (survey non-converters to understand barriers)

Secondary Opportunity: London Techno Expansion

  • Context: High absolute demand but also higher competitive intensity (Resident Advisor, Dice already strong in this segment)
  • Approach: Partner with mid-tier venues (300-500 capacity) where larger platforms under-index
  • Rationale: Differentiate on curation (niche sub-genres like melodic techno) rather than competing head-on with established players on mainstream acts
  • Expected impact: 117 events needed to close gap completely, but start with 30 events in underserved sub-genres (lower risk)

Future: Regional Expansion Strategy

  • Cluster approach: Manchester + Leeds + Sheffield share similar demand patterns and geographic proximity – one promoter network could serve all three
  • Timing: After Birmingham pilot validates model (6-9 months)
  • Risk mitigation: Avoid spreading too thin – better to dominate Birmingham than be mediocre in 5 cities

STRATEGIC RATIONALE (PORTER'S FIVE FORCES)

Birmingham represents a greenfield market entry opportunity with favorable structural characteristics:

  • Threat of new entrants: LOW – Requires local promoter relationships and venue access (takes time to build, creates first-mover advantage)
  • Supplier power (promoters): LOW – Fragmented independent promoters eager for distribution channels (we have negotiating leverage)
  • Buyer power: MEDIUM – Users can switch to competitors, but local presence and curation create stickiness
  • Threat of substitutes: MEDIUM – Free events, non-music entertainment compete for leisure spend
  • Competitive rivalry: LOW – Currently underserved by major platforms

Strategic implication: Move fast to establish presence before competitors notice the gap. However, competitive advantage is not defensible long-term (low barriers mean rivals can copy quickly). Success depends on execution speed and building supplier relationships that raise switching costs.
