Williams Racing · sponsorship propensity
01

Where should the next hour of selling go?

Where should the next hour of selling go?

Commercial impact The commercial team has limited time and a target of new sponsors at £5 million a year and above. I used machine learning to make sure that time went to the companies most likely to sign, ranked weekly inside Salesforce with the evidence behind each one, and analysed which behaviours in a live deal actually move it to signature. The reps now open the week with a list they trust, and the findings on renewals and warm introductions tell them which kind of conversation to have next.

84%
of future sponsors placed in the top decile before their deals were announced; 13.7% precision against a 1.6% base rate.

Fan intelligence platform
02

What do a label's fans actually want?

What do a label's fans actually want?

Commercial impact A label decides merchandise, fan club pricing and tour allocation for a community it cannot see clearly. I pulled five platforms into one place and sorted fans into four types, so the label can see what share of an artist's community are superfans, casual listeners or critics, and price and plan for each artist's community rather than for an average fan. Independent coursework built around a UMG-style brief, not commissioned.

Spotify genre classification
04

Does genre live in the audio?

Does genre live in the audio?

Commercial impact Before a label pitches a track it has to decide which playlists and charts it competes in. I tested whether a track's genre can be heard in the audio or is a choice the label makes. For pop, indie and hip-hop it is a choice, so the analysis gives the marketing team permission to pitch a track into the neighbouring genre's bigger playlists where the audio will not give it away.

0.657
macro-F1 across ten genres against a 0.018 baseline.

Live promoter expansion map
05

Where should an indie promoter go next?

Where should an indie promoter go next?

Commercial impact An independent promoter with a strong London base needs to pick a second city without the budget to research all of them. I scored 21 UK cities on venues, audience, market gap, fit and distance, and put the whole shortlist on one screen with the weights visible. The promoter can see the trade-offs at a glance and change a weight to see how the order moves, instead of arguing from anecdote.

Agentic coding benchmark

Do coding agents know when they're wrong?

Do coding agents know when they're wrong?

Commercial impact Any data team is now being asked to let AI coding agents build its models. I tested whether a clean, high-scoring run from an agent means the model underneath is sound. It does not, and the agents did not catch their own failures. The benefit to a business is a simple rule for using these tools: a human checks the model before an AI-generated number goes in front of a decision-maker.

−0.625
R² shipped by the highest-scoring tool, without raising an error.

London air quality pipeline
07Group

What drives London's air quality?

What drives London's air quality?

Commercial impact Seven public datasets that disagreed on format, borough names and time periods, and a question that could only be answered once they were joined without error. I built the layer that made them trustworthy, with checks that stop a bad join before it reaches the analysis. A large venue or a label has this problem every week. Ticketing, streaming, social and merch data all arrive in different shapes, and one silent mismatch in the join means the release-week report or the settlement figures are wrong and nobody knows. This is the work that makes every number after it believable, and it is the part most teams skip.

hourly readings cleaned and schema-checked in Spark.
