Which users will stop buying tickets?

A Random Forest classifier on 12,400 synthetic users predicting 90-day churn, with the decision threshold chosen to minimise business cost rather than to maximise accuracy.

RoleIndividual, self-directed practice
StackPython, pandas, scikit-learn
Data12,400 generated users, Aug to Nov 2023 cohorts
Practice project. The dataset is synthetic and the scenario is fictional; no client was involved. Kept here because the method (cost-weighted threshold tuning on an imbalanced classifier) is one I use in real work.

Problem

A fictional event-discovery platform acquires users at £55 each and loses 11.2% of them within 90 days, churn being no purchase for 90 or more days after the first ticket.

The brief was to flag likely churners early enough to intervene with a discount, and to decide how aggressive that flagging should be.

Skills

  • Point-in-time features: measured at day 60 to predict day 90: days since last purchase, email open rate, search count, tickets bought, support tickets, account age
  • Imbalance handling: class_weight='balanced' (churners weighted about 7.9×) rather than SMOTE, which interpolates patterns not in the data
  • Chronological split: train on Aug to Oct 2023 cohorts, test on Nov 2023
  • Cost function: a missed churner costs £340 of lifetime value, a wasted discount £15, a 23:1 ratio
  • Threshold sweep: the decision threshold chosen from the cost curve, not the 0.5 default

Results

0.32cost-optimal threshold: 37% precision, 77% recall, against 64% and 71% at 0.5
11%lower total cost on the test set, £31,400 down to £27,900
68%churn probability for users inactive 30 to 45 days, against an 8% baseline

Where the value came from

Accuracy is the wrong metric here: predicting "everyone stays" scores 88.8% and catches nobody. The threshold is a business decision expressed as a number, and moving it from 0.5 to 0.32 is where most of the value came from, not from the model itself.

Limits. The intervention maths assumes a £15 discount recovers about 70% of flagged churners, and the whole saving rests on that assumption. The test window is a single month, so seasonality is untested. On synthetic data the feature effects are artefacts of the generator; the point is the workflow, not the coefficients.
