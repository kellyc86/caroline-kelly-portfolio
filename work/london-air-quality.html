Does the ULEZ show up in London's air?

A Medallion pipeline that joins seven London datasets into a borough-month fact table, built to test whether the ULEZ, weather, tree cover and deprivation explain differences in NO2 and PM2.5.

RoleGroup of four. I owned pipeline_silver.ipynb, the Spark cleaning of the 102 MB LAQN readings file
StackPython, Spark, Parquet, DuckDB, MongoDB, TinyDB, GeoPandas, SciPy
DataSeven sources: LAQN hourly readings and episodes, Open-Meteo, IMD 2019, GLA tree canopy 2024, TfL congestion charge counts, ULEZ boundary
CodeGroup repository, private. My Silver-layer notebook available on request.
Group project. The repository is hosted by a group member. The Bronze and Gold layers, the document stores and the analysis were shared across the four of us.

Problem

Poor air quality is considered the greatest environmental risk to public health in the UK. The open data exists, but these datasets are often distributed across multiple platforms and formats, so a key challenge lies not only in analysing the data but in engineering pipelines capable of collecting, transforming and integrating data sources.

The question underneath: did stations inside the ULEZ show lower NO2 in 2024?

Skills

  • Spark cleaning: ~925,000 hourly readings, schema enforcement, type conversion, Title Case borough names as join keys
  • Validation gates: three automated checks on row counts, join-key null rates and borough name consistency before any Gold computation
  • Medallion architecture: Bronze raw, Silver Parquet, Gold DuckDB SQL over Parquet
  • Document stores: TinyDB and MongoDB for variably shaped episode records
  • Spatial and statistical tests: GeoPandas point-in-polygon, Welch's t-test, Pearson correlation

Results

22.85 vs 26.57mean NO2 μg/m³ inside against outside the ULEZ, directionally consistent but not significant (p = 0.121)
+1.36μg/m³ NO2 on atmospheric inversion days
r = −0.026tree canopy against PM2.5; deprivation NO2 difference p = 0.841

The pipeline

All downstream stages read from Silver rather than re-processing Bronze, which keeps transformations auditable.

BronzeRaw pulls from APIs, scrapes and downloads, stored as received
SilverSpark cleaning of seven sources to independent Parquet datasets, three validation checks
NoSQLPollution episodes and debates in TinyDB and MongoDB
GoldDuckDB joins to a borough-month fact table
AnalysisULEZ classification, inversion flag, correlation and t-tests
Limits. Only one monitoring station fell outside the ULEZ boundary in 2024, so the comparison is statistically inconclusive. PM2.5 readings are available for only 37 per cent of borough-month combinations, making borough-level correlation analyses unreliable. The proposed next step is a 2023 versus 2024 difference-in-differences around the August 2023 ULEZ expansion.
