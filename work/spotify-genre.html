Predictive analytics · UCL MSIN0097 · March 2026

Can genre be predicted from audio alone?

When more structure means less signal: gradient boosting on 13 Spotify audio features, and an autoencoder hypothesis that was tested and rejected with a mechanistic explanation.

RoleSole author, individual coursework; agent scaffolding reviewed through a 15-row decision register
StackPython, scikit-learn, LightGBM, PyTorch, pandas, Matplotlib, Google Antigravity
DataKaggle Spotify Tracks (CC0): 10,000 tracks, 10 genres, 1,000 per class, no missing values
Codegithub.com/kellyc86/msin0097-predictive-analytics

Problem

Musical genre is one of the most commercially consequential labels attached to a track: it shapes playlist placement, licensing decisions and algorithmic recommendation. Can it be predicted reliably from audio alone?

The central hypothesis was that a learned autoencoder representation would outperform raw audio features. Three predictions were registered before any modelling, and all three held.

Skills

  • Evaluation design: 70/15/15 stratified split, test set locked and accessed once, StandardScaler fitted on train only
  • Model selection: 5-fold CV on macro-F1 across dummy, LogReg, Random Forest, MLP and LightGBM; paired t-test (t = 3.657, p = 0.022)
  • Tuning: GridSearchCV over learning rate, num_leaves and n_estimators
  • Representation learning: PyTorch undercomplete autoencoder with a bottleneck sweep over {4, 6, 8, 10}
  • Interpretation: permutation importance, PCA of the latent space, calibration curves, confusion matrix
  • Agent governance: three agent errors caught by human review, none visible to automated testing

Results

0.6565test macro-F1 for LightGBM on raw features, against a dummy baseline of 0.018
-0.0975F1 delta for the autoencoder representation: hypothesis rejected
3×popularity's permutation importance (0.192) over the next feature, liveness (0.063)

Figures

MSE reconstruction learns a maximum-variance projection, not a maximum-discriminability one: PC1 captures 78.08 per cent of latent variance against 28.2 per cent for raw features.

Limits. A variable that responds to platform promotion rather than acoustic content is endogenous: popularity's contribution may not generalise across time windows or catalogue slices, so the model should be treated as temporally fragile. The dataset is a single snapshot and cannot account for genre drift. Per-class test F1 spans 43 percentage points, from classical (0.930) to indie (0.498).
